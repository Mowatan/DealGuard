# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Buyers, sellers, and their advisors transacting where money and the transfer of an asset
(real estate, vehicles, equipment, shares, freelance/services) happen at different times, so
each side carries counterparty risk. They arrive on the landing page skeptical: they are
about to trust a third party with deeds or funds, often for the largest transaction of their
year. They are not crypto-natives. Many are in Egypt / MENA (pricing is in EGP). Their job on
this page: understand in under a minute what DealGuard holds, how release is controlled, and
whether it is safe enough to start a deal.

## Product Purpose

DealGuard is a digital escrow **governance** platform. It does not silently automate money;
it structures the deal into evidence-backed milestones, verifies proof, and gates release
behind human approval, with a full audit trail. Three service tiers: Governance Advisory
(verify only), Document Custody (hold deeds/titles), Financial Escrow (hold and disburse
funds). AI suggests structure and maps evidence; humans approve every decision. Success on the
landing page = a qualified visitor starts a deal or picks a tier, trusting the process.

## Brand Personality

Modern and precise, with approachable startup warmth. Voice: plainspoken expert. It explains
a serious, high-stakes process without intimidation and without hype. Confident, exacting,
human. Three words: **precise, trustworthy, clear.** The emotional goal is earned calm: "I
understand exactly what happens to my money, and a person signs off before anything moves."

## Anti-references

- Generic indigo/violet-on-near-white "AI SaaS template" landing pages.
- Crypto/web3 aesthetics: neon gradients, glassmorphism, "blockchain startup" energy (there
  is a blockchain feature, but it is a footnote, not the identity).
- Navy-and-gold "legacy fintech / private bank" cliche (the second-order fintech reflex).
- Endless identical icon + heading + text card grids; emoji used as iconography.
- Fear-based or hypey escrow marketing.

## Design Principles

1. **One system, no seams.** Every section speaks the same color, type, and spacing language.
   A visitor should never feel the page change hands. (The current page literally switches
   design systems mid-scroll; this is the cardinal sin to fix.)
2. **Show the mechanism.** Trust comes from seeing how release is controlled, not from the
   word "secure." Favor a concrete diagram/visual of the milestone→evidence→approval→release
   flow over decorative cards.
3. **Plain words over jargon.** Say what literally happens ("a person approves before funds
   move"), not "enterprise-grade infrastructure."
4. **Calm by default, warm at the decision points.** Restrained surface; warmth and color
   energy concentrated where the visitor acts (CTAs, the recommended tier).
5. **Mobile is the real page.** Most first visits are on a phone; design the small screen
   first and let it scale up.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text >= 4.5:1, large text >= 3:1, placeholders included; no information
carried by color alone (tier "included/not included" needs icon + text). Full keyboard
operability for nav, accordions, and the tier cards. Every animation has a
`prefers-reduced-motion` fallback. Targets >= 44px on touch.
